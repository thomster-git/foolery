Sports & Team Backings

Personal histories, team loyalties, physical scoresheets, and why I back the teams I do.

▼

Baseball (MLB) — Toronto Blue Jays

I'm a fan of the game first, not just the data. There is nothing quite like watching a live ballgame with a pencil in hand, filling out a physical scoresheet pitch by pitch as each inning unfolds.

I follow the Toronto Blue Jays today, but I didn't always back them. Growing up as a kid, I was actually a New York Yankees fan—mostly for Derek Jeter and my copy of All-Star Baseball 2004 on the PlayStation 2 that my great-grandfather gifted me for my birthday one year. I used to play baseball myself before getting involved with air cadets. Back then, my dad emailed a few MLB teams asking for local team sponsorships, and the Yankees actually sent us a bunch of official hats, which felt incredible at the time.

I eventually walked away from New York, though. Over time I saw the NYY interlocking logo being worn everywhere by people who didn't even watch baseball, and I had zero desire to be lumped into that group: people who just wear hats for the logo. When I returned to baseball in 2020, I started backing the Blue Jays. They're the team I am physically closest to, Canada's representative team in the league, and an obvious home. I've been to games at the SkyDome (Rogers Centre), have collected a bunch of Blue Jays memorabilia, and follow the 162-game campaign through every high and low.

Hockey (NHL) — Montreal Canadiens (Habs)

I have a very similar background and story for why I watch the Habs. As a kid in the late 90s and early 2000s, I was originally a "fan" of the Detroit Red Wings during their dominant dynasty run, drawn in by their star-studded roster before developing my own authentic regional connection.

Today, I bleed Bleu-Blanc-Rouge for the Montreal Canadiens. The unmatched historic prestige, the passionate culture of the Bell Centre, and the current rebuilding era with an electric young core make every matchup essential viewing. Since coming fully back to hockey in 2024, I tune in night after night to follow the player developments, defensive schemes, and the relentless 82-game journey.

American Football (NFL) — Buffalo Bills

I've watched the Super Bowl every single year for well over a decade, but for a long time I never had a singular NFL franchise that I followed week in and week out.

Since coming back to hockey in 2024, I decided I wanted an NFL team to follow full time as well—so I picked the Buffalo Bills. Being physically right across the Canadian border, having one of the most passionate, authentic, and loyal fanbases anywhere in professional sports (Bills Mafia), and getting to watch Josh Allen command cold-weather winter football made Buffalo the absolute natural choice.

Formula 1 — Motorsport Passion

I started watching Formula 1 in 2023, and it wasn't because of Drive to Survive. My friends had been telling me about the sport for a few years at that point, and at the start of the 2024 season I finally tuned in—including all of preseason testing. I was hooked immediately.

I watched almost every practice session, qualifying, and race that year. Towards the end of the 2024 season, I was rooting hard for McLaren during their title fight. In 2025, I started rooting for Oscar Piastri over Lando Norris, while remaining a fan of Max Verstappen's precision and Charles Leclerc's raw one-lap pace. Looking ahead to 2026, I'm officially becoming a Lewis Hamilton fan as he takes on the storied legacy in Ferrari scarlet.

Video Games & Mechanical Systems

Significant eras, defining memories, mechanical deconstructions, and personal history across 25+ years of gaming.

▼

Nintendo 64 Era (1998–2002)

The era where my gaming foundation was cemented. Stepping into Hyrule in The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time was a transformative experience—not just for the story, but for the mechanics. Memorizing the 12 songs note-for-note on the N64 trident controller, the eerie acoustic atmosphere of the Forest Temple, navigating the fluid puzzle mechanics of the Water Temple, and learning how melodic phrasing could shape game design.

It was also the golden age of living room couch multiplayer: four tangled controller cords, Super Mario 64, GoldenEye 007, and Super Smash Bros., where the joy came from being in the same room sharing the screen.

Game Boy Era (2000–2011)

My constant handheld companion across childhood and adolescence. This era was defined by a deep obsession with Pokémon from the very beginning: waking up early on weekends to trade via link cable on Red, Blue, and Yellow, the massive leap into Gold, Silver, and Crystal with real-time day/night cycles, and sinking hundreds of hours into Ruby, Sapphire, Emerald, and FireRed. Beyond Pokémon, it was tactical turn-based mastery on the go with Advance Wars and the puzzle-solving brilliance of Golden Sun.

This era came to an emotional close around 2011 with the onset of severe eye troubles (corneal conditions and surgeries), making small, backlit handheld screens physically painful to focus on and shifting my gaming journey toward larger displays.

PlayStation 2 Era (2002–2008)

The peak era of deep mechanical systems and epic narratives. Final Fantasy X remains one of the most influential games of my life: the strategic Conditional Turn-Based (CTB) combat timeline, the immense freedom of Sphere Grid pathing, the addictive mechanics of Blitzball, capturing every beast across Spira for the Monster Arena, and the painstaking pursuit of Celestial Weapons.

It was also the home of All-Star Baseball 2004—gifted by my great-grandfather on my birthday—which ignited my passion for baseball tracking and Derek Jeter. Alongside masterpieces like Grand Theft Auto: Vice City and San Andreas, Kingdom Hearts, and Metal Gear Solid, the PS2 set my standard for mechanical depth.

⚔️ Live Tool: FFX 100% Completion Tracker →

Xbox 360 Era (2008–2012)

The online multiplayer revolution and the high-water mark of late-night camaraderie with friends. Halo 3 was the uncontested king: custom games in Forge, Infection on Foundry, Fat Kid, Duck Hunt, midnight campaign co-op to find hidden skulls, and the golden age of Xbox Live party chats.

It was also the prime of Call of Duty 4: Modern Warfare and Modern Warfare 2—the relentless prestige grind, clutch 1v4 Search & Destroy rounds, and the competitive thrill of chasing Gamerscore achievements across Gears of War. That four-year stretch captured an unforgettable sense of community and competitive energy that defined my high school years.

PC Gaming Era (2011–2025)

Transitioning into custom desktop builds, high-refresh displays, and deep tactical team play. I gravitated toward hero shooters and tactical FPS like Overwatch and Counter-Strike—obsessing over ultimate economy tracking, situational counter-picking, and crisp vocal team coordination. It was also an era of sandbox survival crafting and modding communities.

This chapter came to a temporary pause in 2025 when I repurposed my primary gaming rig into a dedicated Proxmox home server to power Keel Systems infrastructure and my homelab, though I am actively planning on picking PC gaming back up soon.

RuneScape Era (2004–Present)

The enduring, continuous thread running across more than two decades. From logging in during the transition to RuneScape 2 back in 2004, through the mechanical precision and tick manipulation of Old School RuneScape (OSRS), and now diving deep into RuneScape: Dragonwilds.

I love the long-term grind, player-driven economies, lore-rich questing, and challenging boss encounters. That 20+ year obsession directly inspired the tools I build today on Project Atlas—from the 72-minute dedicated server vault respawn timer to our interactive quest progression logs, regional fishing guides, and server deployment manuals.
