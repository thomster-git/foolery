Methodology: 3D Statcast Pitch Kinematics & Rulebook Strike Zone Collision

The 3D MLB Umpire Simulator models pitches from a 54-foot release point ($z_0 = 54.0\text{ ft}$, $y_0 = 6.0\text{ ft}$) down to home plate using constant-acceleration kinematic equations calibrated to MLB Statcast pitch-tracking averages. Each pitch type—4-Seam Fastball (95 mph, $+17.5^{\prime\prime}$ induced vertical break), Slider (85 mph, $+8.0^{\prime\prime}$ glove-side sweep), 12-6 Curveball (78 mph, $-14.0^{\prime\prime}$ vertical drop), and Circle Changeup (84 mph, $-14.5^{\prime\prime}$ arm-side fade)—follows a realistic 3D trajectory with aerodynamic drag.

Pentagonal Prism Swept-Sphere Strike Evaluation & Shadow Zone

Per Official Baseball Rules, home plate is a 17-inch-wide pentagon ($8.5^{\prime\prime}$ half-width) extending from the front edge ($z = 0$) to the rear apex ($z = -17^{\prime\prime}$). A pitch is a rulebook strike if any portion of the baseball ($1.45^{\prime\prime}$ radius sphere) intersects the 3D pentagonal prism between the batter's knees ($1.5\text{ ft}$) and midpoint of the torso ($3.5\text{ ft}$). Pitches within $\pm 1.45^{\prime\prime}$ of the outer boundary are classified into the Shadow Zone—where MLB umpires and the Automated Ball-Strike (ABS) challenge system face the toughest calls.
