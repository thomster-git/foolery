📊 MLB Benchmark Reference & Scorecard Analytics In a standard 9-inning Major League Baseball game, pitchers throw an average of ~290 to 300 total pitches (~145 per team). Of those, approximately ~140 to 150 pitches are taken (not swung at), requiring an umpire decision. Official MLB Umpire Scorecard analytics reveal that modern big-league umpires average ~94.0% Overall Accuracy (with top-tier umpires reaching 96%+), ~88% accuracy in the Shadow Zone (borderline pitches within ±1.45″ of the edge), and ~97% accuracy in clear Chase/Heart zones, with an average net run favor of ±0.40 to 0.80 runs per game.

Methodology: 3D Statcast Pitch Kinematics & Rulebook Strike Zone Collision

The 3D MLB Umpire Simulator models pitches from a 54-foot release point (z0 = 54.0 ft, y0 = 6.0 ft) down to home plate using constant-acceleration kinematic equations calibrated to MLB Statcast pitch-tracking averages. Each pitch type—4-Seam Fastball (95 mph, +17.5″ induced vertical break), Slider (85 mph, +8.0″ glove-side sweep), 12-6 Curveball (78 mph, −14.0″ vertical drop), and Circle Changeup (84 mph, −14.5″ arm-side fade)—follows a realistic 3D trajectory with aerodynamic drag.

Pentagonal Prism Swept-Sphere Strike Evaluation & Shadow Zone

Per Official Baseball Rules, home plate is a 17-inch-wide pentagon (8.5″ half-width) extending from the front edge (z = 0) to the rear apex (z = −17″). A pitch is a rulebook strike if any portion of the baseball (1.45″ radius sphere) intersects the 3D pentagonal prism between the batter's knees and midpoint of the torso. In MLB Statcast tracking, this vertical zone dynamically scales with batter height and stance—ranging from a compact ~1.34'–3.16' zone for a 5'6" hitter (Jose Altuve) to an elevated ~1.72'–3.94' zone for a 6'7" slugger (Aaron Judge). The 3D batter model and strike zone box dynamically adapt to each hitter's true height and batting box side. Pitches within ±1.45″ of the outer boundary are classified into the Shadow Zone—where MLB umpires and the Automated Ball-Strike (ABS) challenge system face the toughest calls.
