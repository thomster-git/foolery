# Music

Music has always been more than listening. It's a physical experience — bass in the chest, rhythm as a regulatory anchor, specific frequencies that organize my internal state when nothing else does.

Before kids, I logged between 10,000 and 150,000 minutes of music every year. Then parenthood arrived and that number collapsed to zero — replaced entirely by children's songs. I didn't know how important that was until the silence stretched long enough to notice.

The last Billy Talent concert I attended, I quietly cried to myself at one point. The two people with me didn't notice. It wasn't grief. It was an overwhelming recognition of something my nervous system had been missing for a long time.

Now I understand it as stimming. Music is an auditory stim — and for an autistic nervous system, that's not a casual preference. It's a regulation tool.
