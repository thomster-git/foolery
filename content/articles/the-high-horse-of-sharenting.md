# The "High Horse" of Sharenting: A Pragmatic Look at Social Media Privacy

What is my stance on posting personal photos—specifically of my own children—on social media?

Let me start by making one thing perfectly clear: I am not strictly for or against it. If you want to keep your child entirely offline, that is absolutely your prerogative. However, choosing that path doesn't give anyone the right to stand on a pedestal and preach. You aren't inherently better than anyone else because you chose zero online visibility for your kids.

Let's look at the reality of modern technology: if you take photos of your family with an iPhone or an Android, Apple and Google already know you have a child. Your smartphone's data and metadata are already being packaged by tech companies. The idea that avoiding a public profile picture makes you entirely off the grid is a bit of an illusion.

## The Real Threat: Real-Time Logistics vs. Curated Sharing

I am all for curated, appropriate, and timely photos on social media. What I take issue with isn't the photos themselves, but the oversharing of real-time logistics.

The real danger isn't posting a curated family portrait; it's posting that your house is going to be empty for a week while you're on vacation, on the very day you leave.

I get annoyed when family members—like my mom—post that she has my son for the week right after the first day. It broadcasts location and routine in real-time. I wouldn't mind at all if she posted a great album of their time together after he was safely back home with us. I feel the exact same way when I see friends posting day-by-day vacation updates while they are still hundreds of miles away from home.

## Finding the Middle Ground

Even massive public figures navigate this balance. Take PewDiePie, for example. He's incredibly famous and naturally faced massive security considerations. Initially, he avoided showing his son's face completely, but eventually, he started sharing curated, intentional pieces of his family life.

It’s about being smart, not paranoid.

There is a massive difference between chronic oversharing with real-time location data and simply having a nice photo of your family as your profile picture. We need to stop equating the two and let go of the judgment. Share the memories, wait until you get home to post the vacation album, and let's all step down from the privacy pedestal.
