> **Reflecting on early sensory memories, vivid nightmares, and watching my parents play Super Nintendo.**

Looking back at preschool and early childhood, certain memories remain vivid—not always because of big events, but because of the intense sensory and emotional impressions they left behind.

## The Super Nintendo Era

Some of my fondest early memories involve sitting in our living room watching my parents play Super Nintendo. My dad and mom would play through *The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past* and *Donkey Kong Country*. Because I wasn't skilled enough as a small child to navigate the trickier levels, watching them solve puzzles and explore side-scrolling worlds became my earliest introduction to gaming culture and music.

## Early Sensory Panic & The Dryer Incident

I recall an early experience playing hide-and-seek where I hid inside our clothes dryer. My uncle jokingly held the door shut from the outside. While meant as a harmless tease, the sudden loss of control and enclosed space triggered a sharp wave of claustrophobic panic. I thought I was locked in forever and wouldn't be found. When the other adults saw how upset I was, they were very empathetic, but the incident instilled a lingering sense of trust issues. The only other time I felt a similar panic growing up was when a friend of my parents used to grab me and hold me upside down.

Around this time, I also experienced my first pet loss when our cat Patches suffered a seizure on the kitchen floor (we suspected lead paint or mercury exposure from outside). As a preschooler, I didn't know how to process or express grief, resulting in a quiet, internal shutdown.

## Reoccurring Nightmares & The Floating Entity

Throughout early childhood, I experienced persistent reoccurring nightmares. The dreams frequently ended with a menacing, floating bald head with wide eyes and a creepy, toothy smile. It would stare directly at me, nodding. It never actually spoke out loud, but I could always hear its voice in my head asking: *"That was a scary dream, wasn't it? Want to watch it again?"* 

Usually, that was the moment I would wake up in a panic. I remember one specific time when I decided to just continue sleeping. As predicted, the entity restarted the loop, and I was forced to watch the exact same nightmare all over again. 

When my grandmother gave me a traditional dreamcatcher to hang above my bed, the nightmares dramatically dropped by over 80%. Whether psychological grounding or comforting belief, it offered my young mind a much-needed sense of safety.
