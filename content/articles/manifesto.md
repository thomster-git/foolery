# The Project Atlas Manifesto

Welcome to **Project Atlas**. 

If you clicked the central node on the interactive map, you're probably wondering what all of this is. The short answer is: this is a digital garden. The longer answer is that this is an experiment in mapping the human mind and the connections that form between our varied interests.

## Everything is Connected

We live in an age of silos. Social media profiles capture our fleeting thoughts. GitHub captures our code. LinkedIn captures our sanitized professional history. Spotify captures our taste in music. But none of these platforms capture *us*. They don't capture the connective tissue between *why* we build a certain project and the *book* we read that inspired it.

**Project Atlas was built to tear down those silos.**

This site is not a blog, and it's not a resume. It is a living, breathing map of my digital brain. Every article, every piece of software I've written, every book I've read, and every tool I use is a **node**. And every relationship between them is an **edge**. 

- If I write an article about AI infrastructure, it connects directly to the Python projects I've built.
- If I review a sci-fi book, it connects to the themes of futurism and the video games I play.
- If you look at my Resume, you aren't just seeing a list of jobs—you are seeing the hub that connects to the actual open-source projects I shipped during those roles.

## The Architecture of Thought

By using a localized, static-generated graph database, this site visually represents how ideas cross-pollinate. When you explore the interactive directory on the homepage, you are essentially flying through a 2D projection of my interests.

I encourage you to wander. Click a node, open the side panel, and follow the **Connected Content** down the rabbit hole. You might start by looking at my Twitter links and end up reading an essay about system architecture.

That is the beauty of the Atlas. **Everything is connected.**
