## How EtchCentric Started

EtchCentric Creations grew out of an intersection I didn't plan. It actually started because my partner launched a cake pop side business. I found myself taking the advertisement photos, mixing the dough, and helping bake. In my opinion, we made the best cake pops — they were delicious. But the business was incredibly hard on her. She was pulling all-nighters to fulfill orders, often procrastinating due to undiagnosed ADHD, and her perfectionism meant the time required far exceeded what people were paying for. 

At this point, I had been out of a job for two years and wasn't getting any emails back from my old employer, CFI. I started doing some research and thought a laser engraving and cutting business would be a solid venture to pursue. Ironically, just two months after I bought the laser, I got a job back out at the mine. Because of that, I only actively did sales up until the end of that year, including one Christmas Craft fair. But in that short amount of time, I had a pretty successful bout of sales — lots of learning, long nights, trial, and error.

The **xTool S1 40W Diode Laser** is not a toy. It's a precision instrument that requires calibration, material understanding, and patience. Getting good results isn't about finding one universal setting and applying it everywhere. Different materials behave completely differently under the same laser parameters. Wood, slate, leather, anodized aluminum, acrylic — each has its own response to speed, power, frequency, and focus distance.

---

## The Material Test Grid: An Engineering Approach to Craft

For every new material, the first thing I run is a **material test grid**.

A test grid is a matrix of engravings — systematically varying speed and power across rows and columns — so you can visually assess how the material responds across a full range of settings in one pass. You're looking for the combination that gives you the contrast, depth, and edge definition you want without burning through the material or leaving it too shallow to read.

This approach came naturally from an engineering mindset. You don't guess and iterate blindly — you design an experiment, observe the results, and let the data tell you where to refine.

## The Community: Collaboration and Competition

One of the things that makes diode laser engraving genuinely accessible is the community that has formed around it. There are dedicated Facebook groups where hundreds of makers share material-specific settings, post their results, troubleshoot each other's problems, and collectively build a knowledge base.

However, the local community aspect wasn't without its friction. When I first started EtchCentric, a girl who lived in my area and ran her own home business (mostly vinyl, though I think she bought a laser around the same time) suddenly blocked my fiancée and her sister from her Facebook group. She had been established for a few years, and my fiancée had previously purchased items from her (and vice versa with the cake pops). I'm still not entirely sure what the reasoning was, but it didn't feel very fair. Her page was named "Triple K," which I have... feelings about, but that's a story for another time.

## What I'm Making

EtchCentric's focus has been on custom, physical creations with a sense of permanence. In my brief but intense stint doing sales, I worked with several local businesses and individuals to create:

- **Branded Merchandise:** Custom keychains, coasters, and bottle openers for local businesses.
- **Fire Department ID Tags:** Durable, high-contrast identification tags for a local fire department.
- **Acrylic Headstones:** A custom memorial headstone for a passed cat.
- **Song Lyrics Art:** A custom anniversary present featuring engraved song lyrics.
- **Rustic Wooden Signs:** Collaborated with my fiancée to make circular, rustic wooden signs for Halloween and Christmas.
- **Slate & Wood Coasters:** Natural slate pieces — landscapes, portraits, logos, and original designs. Slate has a permanence to it that other materials don't.

---

## What's Next

The craft is still evolving. Better design skills, more material experimentation, and a growing catalog of original designs rather than purely custom commissions. I had a lot more ideas and things in store for EtchCentric, but work for CFI got busy again. Still, the test grid mentality carries into every new project — methodical, curious, willing to fail on purpose in a controlled way so the final piece lands exactly right.
