I quit my job today. No 10-step thread, no 'I'm built different' grindset. Just a tired nerd trying to figure out what's next. 

Looking back, CFI shaped my career in ways I never expected, for better and for worse. The management might have been absurd at times, and the ending of my first run was a disaster, but I wouldn't have found my way into tech without it. However, the environment eventually pushed me to the edge.

## The Breaking Point

The straw that finally broke the camel's back came down to two major incidents.

The first was a manufactured conflict. The day I quit, my supervisor pulled me aside, asking about an ongoing conflict between me and a senior engineer. I genuinely didn't know what he meant. As it turned out, the evening prior, that engineer had called my supervisor claiming I was "ignoring his emails" and attempting to get me in trouble and face consequences for not replying to his message asking how to submit a ticket. 

The reality? I had sent an email answering this in the past, and his new message felt entirely like a "cover my ass" tactic rather than a genuine ask for help. He never followed up with me. No Teams message. No direct call. Just a single email and an immediate escalation directly to my supervisor on a Wednesday evening to try and weaponize my lack of instant response against me.

The second incident was even more personal and deeply damaging. I found out how my autistic meltdowns were being discussed behind my back by management. Instead of any semblance of support or understanding, the consensus was that I should just "fucking calm down!" 

## Toxic Culture and Taking Leave

Looking back, maybe I should have taken sick leave to recover from the burnout and the meltdowns. But the culture at CFI was so toxically ingrained that taking leave wasn't a safe option. 

Another employee who was on sick leave at the time was openly ridiculed. She was mocked behind her back, with people assuming she was just taking leave "to prove a point." Knowing that this was how vulnerability and health crises were treated, I realized there was no space for me to recover or exist authentically in that environment. 

So, I walked away. It was terrifying, but realizing that my mental health and neurodivergence were actively being mocked made the choice clear. I couldn't stay in a place that refused to see me as human.
