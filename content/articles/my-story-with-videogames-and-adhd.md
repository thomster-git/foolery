People who don't understand ADHD often ask how someone can struggle to sit through a thirty-minute status meeting and then spend six straight hours routing a 100% completion checklist in *Final Fantasy X*, *Grand Theft Auto V*, or *Old School RuneScape*.

The difference isn't laziness. It is **feedback loop clarity**.

</div>

## Clear Rules and Immediate Feedback

In a lot of everyday corporate work, the rules are fuzzy, the goalposts shift mid-week, and you don't see the result of your effort for months. An ADHD brain starves for dopamine in that kind of fog.

A well-designed game is the exact opposite:
* Every action gives you immediate, honest feedback—a pitch hits the corner of the strike zone, an ocarina note locks onto pitch, or a 600- millisecond RuneScape game tick resolves.
* Even a massive 200-hour goal is broken down into clear, verifiable sub-steps where you can see your progress bar move every ten minutes.

That realization is why I started building the interactive trackers on Project Atlas—like the [FFX 100% Completion Tracker](/tools/ffx-tracker/), the [GTA V 279-Step Checklist](/tools/gta-v-tracker/), and the [Dragonwilds Progression Companion](/tools/dragonwilds-tracker/)—and why I organize complex real-world engineering projects into the same kind of visible, bite-sized checkpoints.
