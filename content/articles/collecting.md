# Collecting: The Artifacts of My Life

I have always been a collector. Looking back, the things I've gathered over the years aren't just objects; they are physical bookmarks of different eras in my life. Some collections were born from deep obsessions, others from social trends, and some just happened accidentally. 

## The Trading Card Era

My earliest collections started where many kids in the late 90s and early 2000s started: **Pokémon** and **Yu-Gi-Oh!** cards. I was deeply invested, but the collection met a sudden, tragic end when our elementary school banned bringing them in. Without the ability to trade, battle, and show them off on the playground, I lost the chance to actually *play* with them, and my interest eventually faded.

## Magic, Money, and Fedoras

As I got older, my collecting habits shifted to reflect my changing interests and environments.
- **Decks of Playing Cards**: Sparked by a fascination with magic, I started collecting different colored Bicycle decks, themed decks, and unique sets from souvenir shops. 
- **Currency & Coins**: This was a hobby that trickled down from my father's side of the family. There's a tangible history to old money that always appealed to me.
- **Fedoras**: A very specific era of my life. I started collecting these during my time at cadet camp in Debert, Nova Scotia. I would buy one or two every summer I went, turning them into a signature piece of my identity during those teenage years.
- **Monster Energy Cans**: For a brief, chaotic teenage period, I tried to collect every flavor and can variation of Monster Energy I could find. It ended predictably—they got moldy, and the collection was thrown out. 

## Sports Memorabilia: Searching for a Team

My relationship with sports—and the memorabilia that came with it—has always been heavily influenced by the people around me and the video games I played.

### Hockey: The Junk Wax Era and The Habs
I have a solid collection of hockey pucks (both bought and gifted from vacations) and some old "junk wax" era hockey cards. In the early 2000s, I remember rooting for the Detroit Red Wings simply because I was at a friend's birthday party and the game was on. Eventually, I gravitated toward the Montreal Canadiens, largely because of Michael Ryder, a fellow Newfoundlander. 

However, by the time I hit Grade 6, I completely stopped following hockey. Since I didn't actively *play* hockey, the kids who did never took my opinions seriously or entertained conversations about it. The gatekeeping killed the passion, and the card collecting stopped.

### Baseball: From Jeter to the Jays
My baseball fandom followed a similar path of self-discovery. When I was younger, I was a massive Derek Jeter and New York Yankees fan. Why? Probably because whatever baseball video game I was playing at the time had them rated as the best overall team. 

But as I got older, I realized that wearing a Yankees hat had become a fashion statement popularized by 50 Cent, rather than a symbol of actual baseball fandom. I hated feeling like a poseur, so I switched my allegiance to the Toronto Blue Jays. As a Canadian, it made perfect sense, and it felt authentic.

I've been to two or three Blue Jays games in my life. During one of those trips, I hit the jackpot for a young collector: I got a team pack of cards, a signed baseball from the current roster, and a pristine 1993 World Series autoball. 

## The Accidental Video Game Collection

Finally, there are the video games. I have a massive video game collection, but ironically, I've never bought them *for the purpose of collecting*. My collection is huge simply because, for over a decade, the easiest answer to "What should we get Jonathan for his birthday/Christmas?" was a video game. They are less of a curated collection and more of a playable timeline of my life. 

***

*Collecting isn't just about amassing things; it's about holding onto the stories, the hyper-fixations, and the communities that shaped who I am today.*
