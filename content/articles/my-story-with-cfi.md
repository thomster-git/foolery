Most IT people never spend a winter in a hardhat checking civil concrete specs, reconciling mine survey data, or planning preventive maintenance for heavy mobile equipment before they get handed the keys to the server room. At Canada Fluorspar Inc. (CFI) in St. Lawrence, I went through almost every one of those roles before becoming IT Coordinator.

Looking back, walking the site from the ground up was the single best preparation I could have had for running its technology.

## Learning How the Mine Actually Moved

When I started out in QA/QC civil inspection and junior project and mining engineering roles between 2017 and 2022—before the company went through receivership and later restarted—I saw how data actually gets created on a mine site. It doesn't start in a clean SQL table. It starts with a surveyor in the wind, a heavy-equipment operator coming off shift, a maintenance planner trying to keep haul trucks running in salt air, and a water-treatment pump that cannot go offline.

When I returned in 2024 on the IT side and stepped into the IT Coordinator role, I already knew what mattered when a cable went dead or a software vendor pitched a cloud system that wouldn't survive a coastal connectivity drop.

## Sixty Endpoints at the End of the Highway

Running IT for sixty endpoints and eight servers four hours down the peninsula from St. John's meant wearing every hat at once: building Python and PowerShell scripts to turn raw gate-swipe CSVs into daily reports, deploying Motive AI dashcams across the fleet, wiring SharePoint and Power Automate purchasing flows, replacing perimeter firewalls, and ultimately rebuilding the entire fleet from bare metal during the Akira ransomware recovery.

It was exhausting, high-friction work in a tough industrial environment—and it taught me more about real-world systems resilience than any textbook ever could.
