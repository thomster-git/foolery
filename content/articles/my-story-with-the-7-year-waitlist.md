Mental health care in Newfoundland has been a complex, incredibly slow journey. 

In the fall of 2015, I sought help for severe depression at a walk-in clinic, receiving a temporary prescription while waiting for a family doctor. By 2017, while working at Dairy Queen, a verbal confrontation with an aggressive customer triggered my first major panic attack. Visiting the psychiatric emergency department led to a medication change that finally brought mental clarity.

```
2015: Initial Depression Treatment (Walk-in Clinic)
  └──> 2017: Dairy Queen Panic Attack & Psychiatric Care
        └──> April 2025: Autistic Burnout & ADHD Evaluation
              └──> Present: Identifying as AuDHD (7+ Year Public Waitlist)
```

The real turning point came in **April 2025**. Following a mental health breakdown and years of severe burnout, I finally received an ADHD evaluation. Starting ADHD medication helped manage executive dysfunction, but an unexpected thing happened: as the mental noise and constant distraction of ADHD quieted down, my Autistic traits became significantly more prominent. 

Today, I live and identify as **AuDHD (Autistic + ADHD)**. In Newfoundland and Labrador, the public health waitlist for an official adult autism diagnosis is currently **7+ years**.

If you are an adult struggling to keep up with the neurotypical world, a 7-year wait for answers is unacceptable. 

> **Systemic Advocacy Note**: If you suspect your child is Autistic, seek early intervention immediately. Early intervention must extend beyond obvious presentations to include **twice-exceptional (2e)** individuals who mask well. Adult diagnostic access in Canada requires urgent, massive reform. 

Navigating the healthcare system for mental health support has taught me that we cannot rely on clinical algorithms alone. We need human empathy and systemic funding, because right now, adults discovering their neurodivergence are being left behind.
