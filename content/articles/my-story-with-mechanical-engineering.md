As I was finishing high school, it was obvious that engineering was the best fit for me when it came to pursuing a higher education. High school maths and physics came easy to me. I’ve always been the type of learner that if I understand a concept, or *why* something is done in a particular way, I just remember it or know how to do it. Rote memorization wasn't my thing, but foundational logic was.

However, my first semester of university was a huge adjustment. It hit me hard, and I actually almost failed out in my second semester. 

At the same time, I was chosen to go to Greenwood, Nova Scotia, to be a staff cadet for the glider program over the summer. It was an incredible opportunity, but I had to make a tough choice: I gave it up to go back to university over the summer and redo the three courses I didn’t "pass" so I could continue the engineering program.

Over that summer, we had to pick our specific engineering disciplines. Even though I was actually "most" interested in computer engineering, I had to be realistic. Due to my specific learning style and the way the courses were being taught and tested at the time, I decided that going into mechanical engineering would be an easier program for me to make it through successfully. Plus, it fit perfectly with my history in LEGO robotics, which had always been a passion of mine.

After that rocky first year and finding the right discipline, the next few years went by relatively easily. I might expand on specific stories in different articles about certain experiences, but that’s the foundation of how I ended up going down the mechanical engineering path.
