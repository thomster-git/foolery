Everyone has one band whose catalogue acts like a timestamp for their entire youth and twenties. For me, growing up in Newfoundland in the 2000s, that band was **Billy Talent**.

From the first time *Try Honesty* and *River Below* came blasting through a pair of cheap computer speakers, there was an urgency in the way they played that sounded completely different from every other four-chord rock band on the radio.

## What Ian D'Sa Does with One Guitar

As someone who spent years playing in school and Air Cadets bands and picking apart melodies by ear, the thing that always hooked me about Billy Talent is Ian D'Sa's guitar work. In most rock bands, one guitarist holds down root-fifth power chords while a second guitarist plays the lead hook on top. D'Sa plays both parts at the same time—weaving frantic, syncopated upper-string melody lines directly into dropped-tuning rhythm chords while singing the harmony vocals.

Two decades later, putting on *Billy Talent I*, *II*, or *III* during a late-night coding session or a long highway drive across the island still hits with the exact same energy it did in high school.
