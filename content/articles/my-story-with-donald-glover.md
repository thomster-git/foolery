It’s hard for me to pinpoint exactly when and where I first discovered Donald Glover. Looking back, it feels less like a singular moment of discovery and more like he just started appearing everywhere I was already paying attention.

The first thing that really caught my eye was Derek Comedy. I was obsessed. I watched every single video they put out, drawn completely into their specific, unpolished style of internet sketch comedy. I loved it so much that I followed them all the way through to their feature film, *Mystery Team*. 

From there, I found Donald’s stand-up comedy specials. I didn't just watch them; I watched them multiple times, practically memorizing the cadence of his jokes. When he made the jump to television on *Community*, it felt like a natural progression. Troy Barnes quickly became one of my absolute favorite characters, largely because it felt like watching the humor I already loved being broadcast on network television.

Then came Childish Gambino. This was yet another creative direction to follow, and I was fully on board. I listened to his debut studio album, *Camp*, constantly. It became the soundtrack to that era of my life. 

When *Because the Internet* dropped, I made a point to experience the album exactly as intended, reading the accompanying screenplay simultaneously as the tracks played. It made it one of the most unique music releases I had ever experienced. Honestly, my ADHD made it incredibly hard to absorb and retain all the details of the script in real-time, but even so, I could still picture parts of the story forming around each song. That experience permanently shaped how I view the album and cemented its place in my mind as a sprawling, ambitious piece of art.

I still appreciate his later work, and I still like his music. *Awaken, My Love!* and everything that followed are undeniable achievements. But somewhere along the line, his output began exploring things outside my specific realm of understanding. 

I’ve spent time thinking about the switch that made the earlier work feel so personal to me. Ultimately, it was the subject matter. Derek Comedy and *Camp* were fundamental for me growing up because I resonated deeply with the content. As he evolved, it wasn't that his work stopped being good, or that resonance failed—it was just that the subject matter moved outside my frame of reference. 

I feel most connected to his earlier career, before the massive mainstream switch. Saying "he stopped feeling like mine" isn't quite right, because artists don't belong to their fans. It's more that the earlier Donald was talking to a version of me that very specifically existed at that exact moment in time. He was creating art for the awkward, internet-obsessed kid I was, and as we both grew up, our paths just naturally diverged.
