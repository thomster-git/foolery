When you spend the first thirty years of your life without knowing you are autistic and ADHD, you end up treating a lot of unspoken social scripts the same way you treat a confusing rulebook at work: you follow the parts that make practical sense, and you quietly wonder why everyone else seems to care so much about the parts that are pure performance.

## Unwritten Rules and Social Scripts

For a lot of autistic people, the word *autigender* isn't about inventing a trend—it is a plain way of describing the fact that autism shapes how you experience *every* social construct, including gender roles and identity expectations. When your brain naturally questions arbitrary rules (*"Why is this expected? Who decided this?"*), you don't experience social categories the same way someone wired for automatic social conformity does.

Learning the vocabulary for that as an adult didn't change who I was at home, in the workshop, or at my desk. It just gave me an honest name for why I had never cared much for performing a role just because a script said I was supposed to.
