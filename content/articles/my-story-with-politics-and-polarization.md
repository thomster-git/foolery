I’ve never been one for team-sports politics. The current climate practically demands that we pick a side, put on a jersey, and accept a pre-packaged set of ideologies without question. I lean centrist, fundamentally rejecting this all-or-nothing approach. But for me, it goes much deeper than just political preference—it's rooted in how my brain works. 

Living with AuDHD means I have a hardwired reflex for justice, fairness, and truth. When things are unfair or lacking nuance, it doesn't just annoy me; it gets deeply under my skin. It affects me profoundly, depending on my surroundings and my current level of over-stimulation.

Take a mundane, everyday example: the grocery store. Imagine I'm standing in line with one kid hanging off my neck and another jumping out of the cart, waiting my turn. Suddenly, a new register opens up. Almost instantly, older folks skitter skatter over there as fast as possible to cut ahead. People—like my mom—will tell me to “just let certain things go.” But I can’t. My brain registers the blatant violation of an unspoken social contract of fairness, and it infuriates me. It isn't just about the grocery line; it's about the principle. 

That same AuDHD-driven demand for truth and accuracy shapes how I consume information. When claims and statements are intentionally skewed or presented unfairly, it triggers that exact same reflex. A perfect example is the often-cited statistic that “women make 78 cents for every dollar a man does.” It frustrates me to no end when politicians wield this number as the ultimate, end-all-be-all proof that systemic sexism is overpowering, while completely ignoring the myriad complex factors that actually go into that figure. When you strip away the nuance to create a convenient weapon, it feels intellectually dishonest. 

Because I demand nuance, I don't fit neatly into either political camp. Take abortion, for instance. I’m not strictly pro-life, but I’m certainly not as freely pro-choice as the far left. I firmly believe it shouldn't be used as a form of birth control, yet I also recognize that it is absolutely necessary for life-saving circumstances and extreme scenarios. A pre-packaged ideology forces you to choose an extreme; reality requires balance.

I apply this same desire for rational fairness to discussions around gender. I believe trans women don't belong in women's sports—it fundamentally breaks the rules of competitive fairness. Furthermore, as I look at modern discussions about gender, I often wonder why people can't just be feminine men or manly women anymore. Even a term like "tomboy" seems to be becoming outdated in today's discourse. I have my own theories and beliefs about gender expression, rooted in the idea that we shouldn't have to overhaul biological categories just to let people freely express who they are.

Ultimately, my centrist leanings aren't born out of apathy. They are born out of a desperate, neurological need for things to make sense, to be fair, and to be true. In a hyper-polarized world that thrives on half-truths and broken rules, demanding nuance might be the most radical thing I can do.
