Some childhood memories stick with you forever, preserved in perfect clarity despite the passing years. This one takes me back to first or second grade—likely first—during a recreational bowling league for kids.

Through the league, I had met a friend who happened to be one of two twin boys in my class. We had bonded over a shared love for the song *Cotton Eyed Joe*, and I always looked forward to playing it on the bowling alley's jukebox. I also loved the pinball machines, making the arcade area my favorite spot to hang out.

One particular evening, I walked up to the jukebox feeling incredibly excited. I had a pocket full of quarters—six of them, to be exact—which meant we could jam out to *Cotton Eyed Joe* a few extra times that night. I walked right up to my friend, who was standing there in his winter coat and hat, reading the song selections. I started excitedly explaining my plan, eager to share the wealth.

But right in the middle of my explanation, I realized something was wrong. To my surprise, it wasn't my friend at all. It was the *other* twin.

As the realization dawned on me, I looked over at the entrance of the bowling alley. There was my actual friend, just arriving and getting his mom to help take off his winter gear. As soon as we made eye contact, he waved at me. 

Instantly, my central nervous system flared up in full fight-or-flight mode. My instinct was to get out of that awkward situation immediately, so I bolted over to him. Meanwhile, my mom was standing next to them, laughing warmly as she realized exactly what had just transpired. 

It didn't cause an emotional outbreak, but the sheer awkwardness and sudden realization cemented the moment in my mind. Even now, the memory of that mix-up at the jukebox is still incredibly vivid.
