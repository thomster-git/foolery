> **Unmasking, reframing thirty years of lived experience, and discovering AuDHD as an adult.**

Receiving a late-in-life diagnosis of ADHD at 31 and Autism at 32 completely reframed how I view my childhood, career, hobbies, and social interactions. It felt like finally receiving the instruction manual to a complex machine I had been blindly operating for three decades.

## The Reality of High-Functioning Masking

For decades, intelligence and heavy adaptation allowed me to mask my neurodivergent traits. In school and workplace environments, I could meticulously analyze expectation patterns and mimic social norms. I learned the scripts for small talk. I learned how to maintain the precise amount of eye contact that signals engagement without tipping into aggression.

But the hidden cost was chronic nervous system exhaustion. High-functioning masking isn't a superpower; it's a massive, invisible background process running on your mental CPU at all times, draining the battery. I would come home from work so depleted that I had nothing left for my personal life or hobbies.

## The April 2025 Turning Point

The turning point happened in April 2025. That was when we were hit with the Akira ransomware cyber attack at work. The sudden crisis shattered the delicate routines I relied on to function.

The inability to balance the sudden, chaotic work life with my personal family life became a massive revelation. I knew I needed to be there for my family, but the immense, unending pressure I felt from my job was extraordinary. The cognitive load exceeded my system's capacity, causing me to experience two extreme autistic meltdowns within a single month. These weren't panic attacks; they were complete neurological overloads where my brain simply shut down to protect itself.

It reached a point where I had to go to the emergency room. Thankfully, they recognized the severity of what was happening and were able to escalate my psychiatrist referral. After speaking with the psychiatrist for just a half-hour, outlining my lifelong struggles with focus, noise, and burnout, they prescribed me methylphenidate.

## When ADHD Medication Cleared the Noise

That first week on medication felt like superpowers unlocking. It was a profound, quiet revelation. I was able to control my appetite without intense cravings overriding my willpower. I was able to stay awake after work until bedtime instead of collapsing from exhaustion. 

Most importantly, the constant noise, the competing radio stations playing simultaneously in my head, and the rambling thoughts finally stopped. For the first time in my life, I could actually concentrate on a task and continue working on it without feeling the desperate, physical need to alt-tab and do something else for quick dopamine hits. The friction between intention and action vanished.

## The Autism Beneath the Surface

After that first week, my body got used to the medication. I struggled significantly when I didn’t have it for a few days between getting refills, a stark reminder of the baseline chaos my brain naturally produces. 

But as time went on, a new, complex reality set in: I noticed my tolerance to outside stimuli was drastically reduced. 

With the ADHD noise gone, the autistic traits underneath became impossible to ignore. It was as if the ADHD had been a loud fan covering up the sound of a ticking clock. Now, with the fan turned off, the ticking was deafening. 

People interrupting me and expecting me to jump up on command, not having complete quiet when trying to think, and other everyday injustices were really starting to agitate me. The unmasking wasn't just a conscious realization—it was a physical inability to continue hiding my discomfort. 

I couldn't mask it as well anymore, and that fundamentally changed how I interact with the world. It forced me to advocate for myself, set rigid boundaries around my energy, and intentionally design the supportive systems in my life to accommodate who I actually am, rather than who I had been pretending to be.
