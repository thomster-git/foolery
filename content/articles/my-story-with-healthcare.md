When I think about my relationship with the healthcare and education systems in 2026, there is one glaring issue that overshadows everything else. 

No doctor, no teacher, and no other childhood mentor ever caught my obvious ADHD and autism. 

It wasn't because the signs weren't there. It was because I had a high IQ, and I learned how to mask my symptoms incredibly well. Because I could perform academically and didn't fit the stereotypical mold of a disruptive or struggling student, the root causes of my exhaustion, sensory issues, and systemic burnout were entirely overlooked. 

## The Metrics of "Success"

The educational system, and by extension the pediatric healthcare system, relies entirely too heavily on catching the kids who are visibly failing by traditional metrics. If a child's grades are slipping, if they are disruptive in class, or if they are entirely non-verbal, intervention is swift. 

But what happens when a child is hyperlexic? What happens when their special interest aligns with academic subjects? What happens when their response to sensory overwhelm is to shut down internally and become the "quiet, easy student" rather than externalizing their distress?

If you are smart enough to compensate for your executive dysfunction—usually through crippling anxiety and adrenaline-driven last-minute panic—you are left to navigate the underlying neurological reality completely on your own. 

## The Mechanics of Masking

Masking isn't just "acting normal." It is a conscious, manual override of your entire nervous system. It means scripting conversations in your head before they happen, suppressing natural stims that help regulate your body, and forcing eye contact even when it feels physically painful. 

For decades, I spent 100% of my social energy just calculating the "correct" response to normal interactions. My doctors saw an articulate, successful young man. They didn't see the hours of isolation required to recover from a single social event. They didn't see the meltdowns behind closed doors when the masking protocols finally broke down.

## The Inevitability of Adult Burnout

The tragedy of the "high-functioning" label (a term I despise) is that it describes how *convenient* you are to the people around you, not how you are actually functioning internally. 

When you spend your entire childhood and young adulthood masking, the debt eventually comes due. For many of us missed by the system, that collision happens in our late twenties or thirties. The sheer cognitive load of maintaining a masked existence while juggling adult responsibilities—career, relationships, finances—becomes unsustainable. 

This leads to Autistic Burnout: a state of physical, emotional, and cognitive exhaustion where the mask completely disintegrates. You lose executive function. You lose the ability to regulate sensory input. 

Only then, when we are completely broken by the effort of pretending to be neurotypical, does the healthcare system finally pay attention. Even then, the initial diagnosis is almost always depression or anxiety—treating the symptoms of the burnout rather than the neurological reality of AuDHD.

That is the fundamental failure of modern healthcare and education when it comes to neurodivergence. We shouldn't have to reach the point of systemic collapse just to get an accurate blueprint of our own minds.
