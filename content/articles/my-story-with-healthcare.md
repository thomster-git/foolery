If you live in Newfoundland and Labrador, you know that the people working on the front lines of our hospitals and clinics are stretched thin, and you also know that if you don't actively track your own referrals, test results, and symptoms, things can slip into a drawer for months.

Between fifteen years of dealing with recurring macular edema, finally getting tested for [severe sleep apnea](/articles/my-story-with-medical-stigma-and-advocacy.html), and sitting beside family members in hospital rooms, I learned early on that you cannot treat a seven-minute medical appointment passively.

## Bringing Telemetry to a Seven-Minute Appointment

When a clinic is running two hours behind schedule, a vague complaint like *"I've been really tired lately"* almost always gets a vague answer.

Walking in with specific, observable data—*"I nod off within three minutes of sitting on the couch, I have had recurring macular fluid since 2010, and I want to rule out obstructive sleep apnea with a Level 3 take-home kit"*—changes the entire conversation. You aren't trying to play doctor; you are giving an overworked clinician the clear signal they need to order the right test the first time.
