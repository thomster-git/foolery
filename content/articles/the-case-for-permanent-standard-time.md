Almost everyone agrees on one thing: **changing our clocks twice a year is a broken, archaic ritual that needs to die.**

Ask ten people on the street if they want to get rid of the semi-annual time shift in spring and fall, and nine of them will say yes before you even finish the sentence. Nobody enjoys reprogramming microwave clocks, wrestling irritable toddlers onto a shifted bedtime schedule, or spending two weeks in a groggy neurological fog because a 20th-century wartime decree decided we should collectively pretend it is an hour earlier or later than the sun says it is.

This autumn—November 2026—several Canadian provinces finally pulled the trigger. British Columbia, Alberta, and Manitoba (joining the Yukon, which made the leap in 2020) officially refused to "fall back," locking their clocks onto **Permanent Daylight Saving Time** year-round.

Ending the biannual clock flip is the right goal. **But locking onto Daylight Saving Time in the fall is the exact wrong execution.**

By refusing to fall back in November rather than waiting until March to simply stop "springing forward," policymakers solved the right problem with the wrong clock—and they did it on the strength of flawed public surveys that confused *hating the clock change* with *wanting permanent summer time in the dead of a Canadian winter*.

If we look at orbital geometry, survey psychology, historical post-mortems, and human circadian biology, the far smarter move was always right in front of us: **wait until spring, skip the "Spring Forward" shift, and stay on Permanent Standard Time forever.**

## 1. The Survey Trap: How Bad Polling & "Spring Forward" Trauma Manufactured a False Mandate

Whenever politicians defend choosing Permanent Daylight Saving Time (DST) over Permanent Standard Time, they point to public consultations—most famously British Columbia’s 2019 online survey, where the government proudly announced that **93% of 223,273 respondents** voted to move to permanent Daylight Saving Time.

In systems engineering and data analysis, whenever a survey returns 93% unanimity on a complex public policy trade-off, your first instinct shouldn't be celebration. Your first instinct should be to audit the questionnaire for **framing bias**.

When you dissect how these provincial consultations and referendums were designed—and *when* they were conducted—three massive methodological flaws jump off the page:

### I. The Double-Barreled Hostage Question
In B.C.’s 2019 consultation and Alberta’s October 2021 referendum, voters were not asked a clean two-step systems question:
1. *Should we eliminate twice-yearly clock changes?*
2. *If yes, should our permanent clock be set to Standard Time (solar alignment) or Daylight Saving Time (summer offset)?*

Instead, Alberta’s ballot asked a single double-barreled question:
> *"Do you want Alberta to adopt year-round Daylight Saving Time, which is summer hours, eliminating the need to change our clocks twice a year?"*

(Even with that loaded wording, Albertans actually rejected the 2021 referendum by a razor-thin **`50.2% No` to `49.8% Yes`**—yet the provincial government still proceeded to lock onto permanent DST in November 2026 anyway.)

Meanwhile, B.C.’s June–July 2019 survey framed the choice as a binary referendum between keeping the hated status quo (changing clocks twice a year) or adopting permanent DST—completely omitting **Permanent Standard Time** as a selectable option on the questionnaire. When you tell an exhausted public that the only way to stop changing their clocks is to check the box marked "Permanent Daylight Saving Time," people aren't voting for a 9:45 AM December sunrise. **They are voting to stop changing their clocks.**

### II. The "Spring Forward" Trauma & Survey Timing
Why do people hate the clock change so viscerally in the first place? 

Nobody wakes up on the first Sunday of November furious that they just got an extra hour of sleep. What people universally despise is the **March "Spring Forward" shift**—the annual biological ambush where an arbitrary rule reaches into your bedroom at 2:00 AM on a Sunday and steals an hour of sleep.

That single lost hour in March is not a minor inconvenience; it is a measurable public health shockwave:
* Clinical cardiology studies consistently show a **24% spike in acute myocardial infarctions (heart attacks)** on the Monday immediately following the Spring Forward transition.
* Emergency room admissions, workplace injuries, and fatal morning motor-vehicle collisions jump by **6% to 8%** during the first week of Daylight Saving Time.
* Millions of workers, parents, and students spend the next ten to fourteen days dragging themselves through acute circadian misalignment.

When you survey people during the bright summer months—or right after they’ve been burned by the spring time change—their recency bias is anchored entirely on two things: *"I never want to lose that hour of sleep in March again,"* and *"We are currently on Daylight Saving Time and the weather is warm and sunny, so let's just freeze it right here!"*

### III. The Branding Trojan Horse ("Daylight Saving" vs. "Standard")
There is also a brilliant piece of century-old marketing at play. Calling `UTC-offset + 1` **"Daylight Saving Time"** makes it sound like a biological stimulus package—as if the government is manufacturing extra sunshine and depositing it into your bank account. Calling true solar time **"Standard Time"** makes it sound like the base-model trim on a fleet pickup truck.

People hear "Permanent Daylight Saving Time" and subconsciously picture 8:30 PM July patio weather lasting through November. They do not mentally calculate where the sun actually sits in the sky at the 49th or 53rd parallel in January.

### IV. November 2026: Instant Buyer’s Remorse
We didn't have to wait long to see that survey illusion collide with reality. Within weeks of B.C., Alberta, and Manitoba refusing to fall back on November 1, 2026, the **Angus Reid Institute** polled residents across all three provinces:

* While majorities still agreed with the *principle* of ending twice-yearly clock changes, **preference for Permanent Daylight Saving Time in Alberta immediately cratered by 14 percentage points** (falling from 47% in March down to just **33%** in November).
* A clear plurality of Albertans (**44%**) stated they would actually prefer **Permanent Standard Time** now that they were experiencing pitch-black winter mornings firsthand.
* Between **59% and 65% of residents** across B.C. (59%), Manitoba (64%), and Alberta (65%) reported immediate operational headaches—from smartphones, transit signs, and vehicle dashboards automatically "falling back" anyway, to cross-province calendar desynchronization and brutal morning commutes in the dark.

## 2. You Cannot Legislate Orbital Mechanics: The Start & End of Daylight

Here is the fundamental law of physics that gets lost in every political debate over clocks: **a clock does not create, save, or destroy a single second of daylight.**

Earth’s $23.5^\circ$ axial tilt and your latitude on the globe dictate the exact number of daylight hours you receive on any given calendar date. At the winter solstice around December 21, Canada’s northern geography means daylight is scarce no matter what number is printed on your wristwatch:
* **St. John's, NL ($47.6^\circ\text{N}$):** `8 hours, 26 minutes` of daylight
* **Toronto, ON ($43.7^\circ\text{N}$):** `8 hours, 55 minutes` of daylight
* **Vancouver, BC ($49.3^\circ\text{N}$):** `8 hours, 11 minutes` of daylight
* **Winnipeg, MB ($49.9^\circ\text{N}$):** `8 hours, 05 minutes` of daylight
* **Calgary, AB ($51.0^\circ\text{N}$):** `7 hours, 54 minutes` of daylight
* **Edmonton, AB ($53.5^\circ\text{N}$):** `7 hours, 27 minutes` of daylight

When voters checked the box for Permanent Daylight Saving Time, most never looked at the **start and end of daylight** across both extremes of the Canadian calendar. Let’s look at the actual astronomical sunrise and sunset numbers side by side:

Look closely at what those numbers mean for daily human life in both winter and summer:

### The Winter Morning Catastrophe (Start of Daylight)
Under **Standard Time**, Canadian winter mornings are already tight—sunrise happens between `7:46 AM` and `8:48 AM` across our major population centres, just as kids are walking into school and morning commuters are on the highway.

Under **Permanent Daylight Saving Time**, you push winter sunrise back a full hour:
* In **Vancouver**, the sun does not rise until **`9:05 AM`**.
* In **Winnipeg**, sunrise waits until **`9:24 AM`**.
* In **Calgary**, sunrise is **`9:36 AM`**.
* In **Edmonton** (and across northern B.C., Alberta, and Manitoba), the sun does not clear the horizon until **`9:48 AM`—nearly ten o'clock in the morning!**

For more than three months of the year, elementary school children are waiting for buses and walking across icy intersections in **pitch-black astronomical night**. Construction crews, industrial mine sites, transport drivers, and morning commuters are operating for two to three hours after waking up without a single ray of natural light. And what did we buy in exchange for three months of pitch-black 9:30 AM mornings? Moving winter sunset from `4:20 PM` to `5:20 PM`—an hour when the vast majority of 9-to-5 workers are still sitting at their desks or stuck in bumper-to-bumper traffic anyway.

### The Summer Evening Myth (End of Daylight)
Now look at the other half of the table, because the biggest fear people have about **Permanent Standard Time** is: *"Won't we lose our bright summer evenings?"*

No—because Canada sits at a high northern latitude where summer days are naturally `15.5` to `17+` hours long!
* Even on **Permanent Standard Time**, the June sun doesn't set until **`8:02 PM` in St. John's**, **`8:22 PM` in Vancouver**, **`8:41 PM` in Winnipeg**, **`8:54 PM` in Calgary**, and **`9:07 PM` in Edmonton**—with bright civil twilight lingering another 45 to 55 minutes after sunset (past `9:00 PM` to `10:00 PM`).
* You *still* get four full hours of warm, bright post-work daylight every evening on Standard Time.
* Better yet, on Standard Time in June, the sun actually sets in time for bedrooms to cool down and for children—and adults who have to wake up at `5:30 AM` or `6:00 AM` for work—to naturally produce melatonin before `10:30 PM`, instead of staring at a blazing `10:07 PM` sun with twilight glowing past `11:15 PM`.

## 3. Solar Noon & Circadian Biology: Why Morning Light Is Non-Negotiable

Having lived for thirty years with [severe, undiagnosed sleep apnea](/articles/my-story-with-medical-stigma-and-advocacy.html) before CPAP therapy gave me my life back, I stopped treating sleep as a negotiable lifestyle preference a long time ago. Sleep architecture is foundational human infrastructure. When you mess with the inputs that regulate human sleep across an entire population, things break.

There is a reason why **every single major sleep and chronobiology organization in North America**—including the **Canadian Society for Chronobiology (CSC)**, the **Canadian Sleep Society (CSS)**, and the **American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM)**—has issued formal scientific consensus statements **opposing Permanent Daylight Saving Time** and **endorsing Permanent Standard Time**.

### What Standard Time Actually Represents
Standard Time is not a political invention. It is geographical physics: **Standard Time is defined so that 12:00 PM (noon) roughly matches Solar Noon**—the moment the sun reaches its highest point in the sky, splitting the daylight symmetrically between morning and afternoon.

Daylight Saving Time artificially overrides that geography, shifting your social clock one timezone to the east (`Solar Noon ≈ 1:00 PM` or `1:30+ PM`).

### The Suprachiasmatic Nucleus (SCN) Needs Morning Light
Deep inside the human hypothalamus sits the **suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN)**—our master biological pacemaker. Left in a dark cave without sunlight, the human circadian clock does not run on a neat 24.0-hour loop; it free-runs slightly longer, averaging **`24.15 to 24.2 hours`**.

Because our internal clock naturally drifts later every day, it requires a daily hardware reset—called **photic entrainment**—to stay locked to the 24-hour rotation of the Earth:
1. **Morning Sunlight Advances the Clock:** When high-intensity, short-wavelength (blue) morning sunlight hits specialized intrinsically photosensitive retinal ganglion cells (`ipRGCs`) in your eyes shortly after waking, it sends an immediate electrical signal to the SCN. That signal shuts off residual nighttime melatonin, triggers the morning cortisol awakening response, elevates core body temperature, and starts a 14-to-16-hour biological timer for tonight’s sleep onset.
2. **Evening Light Delays the Clock:** Bright light exposure late in the evening does the exact opposite—it tricks the SCN into thinking the day is still ongoing, suppressing melatonin secretion and pushing your biological sleep window later into the night.

**Permanent Daylight Saving Time attacks human circadian biology from both ends:**
* **In the winter**, it starves the brain of morning sunlight for two to three hours after the alarm clock rings, forcing students and workers to commute and perform cognitively while their brains are still chemically in the middle of biological night.
* **In the summer**, it floods the retina with bright sunlight until 9:30 PM or 10:00 PM, delaying melatonin onset while the alarm clock still demands a 6:00 AM wake-up for work the next morning.

Chronobiologists call this condition **chronic social jetlag**—a permanent mismatch between your biological clock, the solar clock, and your work clock. Large-scale epidemiological studies comparing populations who live on the western edges of timezones (where sunrise and solar noon occur an hour later, mimicking permanent DST) against those on the eastern edges demonstrate that that single hour of delayed morning light is associated with significantly higher rates of:
* Chronic sleep deprivation and Seasonal Affective Disorder (SAD)
* Obesity, Type 2 diabetes, and metabolic syndrome
* Cardiovascular disease and hypertension
* Workplace accidents and fatal morning traffic collisions

And the United States wasn't an isolated case:
* **The United Kingdom (1968–1971):** Britain ran a three-year trial of permanent year-round summer time (*British Standard Time*, `UTC+1`). In northern Scotland—which shares similar latitudes with Edmonton and northern B.C.—winter sunrise didn't occur until `10:00 AM`. After three winters of dark morning commutes and construction injuries, the British Parliament voted overwhelmingly (`366 to 81`) to terminate the experiment.
* **Russia (2011–2014):** Russia abolished clock changes in 2011 by locking onto **Permanent Daylight Saving Time**. After three brutal winters of morning darkness, seasonal depression, and public anger, public approval for permanent DST collapsed to under 30%. In October 2014, Russia admitted the mistake, rolled the clocks back one final time, and locked permanently onto **year-round Standard Time**—where it has remained ever since.
* **Saskatchewan (1966–Present):** Right here in Canada, Saskatchewan has quietly observed the same time year-round for **sixty years** without flipping a single clock, proving that a Canadian province doesn't need biannual time changes to function.

## 5. The Smarter Cutover Protocol: Why We Should Have Waited Until Spring

If you are a systems architect tasked with decommissioning a legacy twice-a-year scheduled job without disrupting your users, *how* and *when* you execute the cutover matters just as much as the end state.

Compare the two ways a province can end the time change:

### Path A: The Fall Cutover (Permanent DST — The 2026 Mistake)
1. **Timing:** You execute the change on the first Sunday of November, right as the northern hemisphere is losing 3 to 4 minutes of daylight every single day heading into the December solstice.
2. **User Impact:** You take away the *only* clock transition people actually like (the autumn "Fall Back" where everyone gets an extra hour of sleep), and you immediately plunge school children, shift workers, and commuters into 9:15 AM to 9:48 AM sunrises for the next four months.
3. **Biological Debt:** You lock the entire population one hour ahead of the sun forever.

### Path B: The Spring Cutover (Permanent Standard Time — The Smarter Move)
1. **Step 1 (November):** Let the clocks **fall back to Standard Time** one final time in November. Everyone gets their extra hour of sleep. Noon on the clock returns to true Solar Noon. Throughout November, December, January, and February, Canadians get every precious minute of morning sunlight as early as orbital mechanics allows.
2. **Step 2 (March):** When the second Sunday of March arrives, **you simply do nothing.** You delete the "Spring Forward" cron job.
3. **User Impact:** Nobody loses an hour of sleep. Nobody suffers a Monday-morning heart-attack spike or circadian hangover. And because the vernal equinox is only two weeks away, the sun is already rising 3 to 4 minutes earlier and setting 3 to 4 minutes later every single day on its own—naturally delivering bright mornings *and* 8:30 PM to 9:00 PM summer evenings without ever touching a clock again.

## The Bottom Line

People didn't vote in droves to wake up two and a half hours before sunrise every winter for the rest of their lives. They voted because they were sick and tired of having their sleep hijacked every March, and they were handed a loaded question that bundled *ending the clock switch* with *Permanent Daylight Saving Time*.

We don't need to pretend noon is 1:00 PM to enjoy a Canadian summer evening, and we certainly shouldn't be sending kids to school in pitch-black 9:15 AM winter darkness to prove a point. 

End the time changes—yes. But align the clock with the sun, listen to the sleep scientists, wait until spring, and **stay on Standard Time.**
