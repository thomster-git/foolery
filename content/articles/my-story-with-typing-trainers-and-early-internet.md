Before social media algorithms and smartphone touchscreens, getting fast on a computer meant one physical skill above everything else: touch-typing without looking down at a beige PS/2 keyboard.

In school computer labs, we started on rows of bulky CRT monitors running early typing trainers—drilling home-row keys (`A S D F` and `J K L ;`) with a cloth or cardboard cover taped over our hands so we couldn't peek at the keycaps.

## MSN Messenger as the Real Crucible

The classroom typing games taught where the keys lived, but the real speed came from the early internet at home: juggling four simultaneous **MSN Messenger** chat windows while trying to type callouts in online games before your character got killed.

When your fingers can finally keep up with the speed of your own thoughts—without the keyboard feeling like a barrier between your head and the screen—writing code, documenting a 3:00 AM server runbook, or drafting an essay stops feeling like a chore and starts feeling like talking.
