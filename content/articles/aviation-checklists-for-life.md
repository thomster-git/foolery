## The Aviation Standard

In 2011, I did my flight training for a private pilot's license at Gander Flight Training through the Air Cadets. The aviation industry is built on systems thinking, but one thing in particular stuck with me: the laminated checklists.

There are checklists for every critical phase of flight: start-up, shut-down, emergencies, pre-flight checks. Some of them you have to memorize, but the philosophy remains the same: in a high-stress situation, it is incredibly easy to freeze up or skip a step. Following the checklist every single time keeps you consistent. It prevents you from going absent-minded, forgetting to check if your elevator is working, and subsequently crashing on takeoff.

## Engineering the Friction out of Daily Life

While I haven't fully implemented it yet, I constantly think about applying this exact framework to everyday life. 

For someone navigating ADHD and executive dysfunction, mundane multi-step processes can feel like a high-stress emergency. The working memory gets overloaded, steps are forgotten, and the task derails. 

As silly as it sounds, a rigid, systemized approach would be profoundly helpful. Imagine a laminated **"Getting Home from the Grocery Store"** checklist:
- Bring kids in
- Put on a show (have a pre-selected list of options to eliminate decision fatigue)
- Get kids a snack (something already prepared in the house)
- Start bringing in groceries
- Unbag groceries and place on the dining room table
- Put away pantry items
- Put away fridge items
- Throw away any old fridge food

It's the exact same logic used to keep a Cessna in the sky. Take a chaotic, multi-step process with a high chance of distraction or failure, and engineer the friction out of it by removing the need to hold the steps in your working memory. The paper holds the memory. You just follow the steps.
