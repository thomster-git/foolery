> **Healthcare is a double-edged sword—capable of delivering life-changing breakthroughs, yet capable of profound systemic coldness when empathy is needed most.**

My experiences with the healthcare system have required constant self-advocacy. From dealing with physical health issues as a child to managing severe sleep apnea, I've had to learn how to fight for the care I needed.

## Childhood Stigma & Early Health Challenges

Growing up, my physical health journey started with weight issues following a toddler illness. Stigma followed me early on. Moving to Carbonear in 1999 at six years old, I distinctly remember playing outside with neighborhood kids when a peer openly laughed and made derogatory comments about my body. Putting on a brave face to keep my cool became a habit early in life.

Beyond weight, physical health issues mounted:
- **Vision Misdiagnosis**: My parents were initially told my eyesight was fine. A second opinion in Carbonear revealed my eyes were only as developed as a two-year-old's, requiring immediate corrective lenses.
- **Macular Edema**: In the summer of 2010, after a routine contact lens follow-up, my left eye suddenly went completely blurry. As the youngest person in the waiting room by four decades, I was rushed to the Janeway Children’s Hospital emergency room. By 2016, vision distortions began affecting my right eye as well.
- **Physical Complications**: Chronic ear infections required repeated antibiotic courses, alongside painful hereditary ingrown toenail procedures.

## The CPAP Breakthrough & Vision Recovery

For years, I suffered from severe, undiagnosed sleep apnea—a condition running on my father's side of the family. My paternal great-grandmother suffered from it while being completely underweight, which highlighted a frustrating systemic issue: medical fatphobia. When my father underwent a sleep study, he had a terrible experience and was simply told to "lose weight" and it would probably resolve. He still refuses to get re-tested despite my continued advocacy, and it breaks my heart knowing his longevity will suffer because of that dismissal. It’s like telling a diabetic person to just watch what they’re eating instead of giving them insulin.

When I finally completed my own sleep study, the results were alarming:

* **Apnea Index**: Over **60 apnea events per hour** (severe sleep apnea).
* **Oxygen Desaturation**: Blood oxygen levels plummeting into the **60s** during sleep.
* **Licensure Risk**: The Department of Motor Transportation mandates that if I don't use my machine, they will suspend my driver's license. 

Before treatment, no amount of caffeine could keep me totally awake and alert on a 4-hour drive. Starting **CPAP (Continuous Positive Airway Pressure) therapy** transformed my life overnight. The extreme daytime drowsiness vanished, and I stopped waking up constantly in the middle of the night.

Remarkably, starting CPAP therapy directly coincided with my long-standing **macular edema going away**. After fifteen years of specialist follow-ups, my ophthalmologist confirmed the edema had fully subsided. Restoring proper nighttime oxygen saturation allowed my eyes to heal.
