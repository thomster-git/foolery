When you do well in math, join Lego Robotics and Air Cadets, earn a glider and private pilot license as a teenager, and graduate with an engineering degree, nobody in the 1990s or 2000s stops to ask if you might be autistic or have ADHD. Back then, as long as your report card marks were high and you weren't flipping desks, any friction you ran into was just labeled as being *"stubborn,"* *"intense,"* or *"scattered."*

## The Tug-of-War Between Autism and ADHD

Understanding **AuDHD** as an adult felt like finding the wiring schematic for a machine I had been operating by trial and error for thirty years.

When Autism and ADHD live in the same head, they pull against each other in ways that look contradictory from the outside:
* The autistic side craves structure, clean documentation, predictable routines, and quiet sensory environments.
* The ADHD side craves novelty, urgent troubleshooting, and deep hyperfocus rabbit holes—and stalls out the moment a task feels repetitive or bureaucratic.

Once you stop fighting that wiring and start designing your work around it—building structured checklists and automated pipelines during high-focus bursts so the routine stays on rails when your attention shifts—thirty years of confusing habits suddenly make complete sense.
