> **High-functioning masking is an intellectual marathon. Brute-forcing a Mechanical Engineering degree without knowing you're neurodivergent works until the internal engine collapses into Autistic Burnout.**

Receiving an ADHD diagnosis at age 31 and an Autism diagnosis at age 32 brought thirty years of unexplained struggles into sharp focus. For three decades, I navigated education, social dynamics, and careers with an undiagnosed neurodivergent brain—relying on high intelligence to compensate for hidden neurological strain.

---

## 1. High IQ & Social Brute-Forcing

Growing up twice-exceptional (2e), masking was not an intuitive social skill; it was a conscious, analytical algorithm:
- **Social Mistake Cataloging**: Whenever I made a social blunder or experienced an awkward interaction, I mentally cataloged the mistake, analyzing the variables so I could explicitly avoid repeating it in future interactions.
- **Academic Brute-Forcing**: Having a high IQ allowed me to "brute-force" my way through primary school, post-secondary education, and earn a rigorous **Mechanical Engineering degree** without formal study systems or early neurodivergent accommodations.

```
High-IQ Masking Algorithm:
  Social Interaction ──> Awkwardness / Mistake ──> Mental Error Analysis
                                                          │
  Academic Success <── Brute-Force Intellect <── Cataloged Behavioral Rule
```

While brute-forcing got me through engineering school and into technical careers, the cognitive overhead of constant real-time social computation was accumulating interest behind the scenes.

---

## 2. When ADHD Calmed Down, Autistic Traits Grew Louder

In **April 2025**, the dam broke. Years of unrelenting compensation culminated in severe **Autistic Burnout**. 

Working with mental health professionals led to initiating ADHD medication. But as stimulant therapy successfully quieted the restless ADHD "noise," my underlying Autistic traits became significantly more prominent:

```
Pre-Treatment (ADHD Restlessness Masks Autism)
  ├── Constant Mental Noise & Impulse
  └── High Sensory Threshold (Chaos Distracts Chaos)

Post-Treatment (ADHD Quieted by Medication)
  ├── 🔊 Sensory Sensitivities Heightened (Bright Lights, Loud Audio)
  ├── 🔒 Rigid Routine Preferences Required
  ├── 🎯 Intense Hyperfocus Tracks
  └── 🔋 Hard Social Energy Caps (Rapid Battery Depletion)
```

Therapy confirmed that I was experiencing classic Autistic Burnout. Today, I am actively working through recovery using resources like *Unmasking Autism* by Dr. Devon Price and *The Autism Burnout Recovery Workbook*, unlearning decades of unhealthy suppression.

---

## 3. Childhood Intuition & Non-Conforming Curiosity

Reading *Unmasking Autism* introduced me to the concept of **Autigender**—how Autistic cognitive processing alters a person's relationship to societal gender expectations and rigid social constructs.

Looking back at childhood, my interests frequently crossed traditional 1990s gender boundaries:
- **The Easy-Bake Oven**: As a child, I desperately wanted an Easy-Bake Oven. It wasn't because I identified as a girl; I simply loved the idea of baking and eating fresh cookies and cakes. My dad refused to get me one, fearing it would make me "gay"—a reflection of rigid 90s gender tropes.
- **Kindergarten Nurturing**: In kindergarten, my favourite activity was taking home the class teddy bear and caring for it—a tender, nurturing trait that defied masculine stereotypes of the era.
- **Anatomical Confusion**: Prior to puberty, pubic fat stores made my anatomy appear smaller, and I noticed the scrotal raphe line. Intuiting as a young child, I thought: *"This line looks like it was sewn together... was I supposed to be a girl?"* At one point I taped my anatomy down, but after using the bathroom realized how silly it was and snapped out of it. 

I didn't think much about that childhood event again for over two decades—until discovering how Autistic children interpret physical bodies and social roles through literal, curious logic.

---

## 4. Autigender, Belonging, & The Risk of Misdiagnosis

Understanding **Autigender** offers vital perspective on modern discussions surrounding identity, youth, and belonging.

```
Isolated / Undiagnosed Autistic Youth (Searching for Belonging)
  ├── Non-Conforming Interests + Literal Body Intuition
  ├── Social Alienation / Lack of Peer Connections
  └── Risk of Medical Misdiagnosis vs. True Neurodivergent Unmasking (AuDHD)
```

As a 32-year-old adult, I reflect on what might have happened had I been born twenty years later in 2013. If an isolated, undiagnosed Autistic kid searching for peer belonging were exposed to online spaces where medical transition is presented as the primary answer to social alienation, I might have been convinced to pursue permanent medical transition.

Looking back now, I recognize that would have been a catastrophic misdiagnosis for what was fundamentally **undiagnosed AuDHD, high-IQ masking, and Autigender non-conformity**.

---

## 5. Unmasking & Moving Forward

True self-acceptance at 32 means embracing neurodivergence without needing to force myself into rigid boxes:
1. **Honoring Social Energy Caps**: Respecting my social battery without feeling guilty for needing solitary recovery time.
2. **Sensory Accommodation**: Designing home lab, studio, and maker environments that accommodate sensory sensitivities.
3. **Open, Nuanced Dialogue**: Advocating for twice-exceptional awareness, early childhood screening, and nuanced conversations about identity that protect vulnerable neurodivergent youth.
