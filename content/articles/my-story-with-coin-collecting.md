> **Holding history in the palm of your hand.**

My journey into coin collecting didn't begin with a grand plan or a sudden burst of numismatic passion. Instead, it started the way many lifelong hobbies do: through gifts and hand-me-downs from family. When I was younger, I was given a few modest but meaningful items that sparked my curiosity. There was a set of 1993 Canadian coins—my birth year—along with a couple of Canadian quarter sets and an old $2 Canadian bill.

At the time, I didn't know much about what made a coin special, let alone what these pieces were actually worth. I didn't have the resources, the reference books, or the internet savvy to look up mintages or historical values. Instead, I just spent hours sorting through them and admiring the physical craftsmanship. There was a certain magic in holding something that felt so permanent, imagining the pockets and cash registers it had traveled through before ending up in my hands. I appreciated the physical history of the coins far more than their monetary value.

As the years went by, what started as a childhood fascination slowly evolved into a more focused and intentional pursuit. I transitioned from merely admiring gifts as a kid to actively studying and categorizing them. Today, my primary focus lies in Canadian Silver Dollars, specifically those minted before 1968, when the Royal Canadian Mint still used 80% silver. There's a satisfying heft and a distinct sound to those old silver coins that you just don't get with modern currency.

But it's not just about hoarding silver. The real thrill for me now is the hunt for the imperfect—finding errors and die cracks. While many numismatists spend hours hunched under a magnifying loupe, my process has been much simpler. I essentially just sort through my old containers of change, doing research on what to look for. I primarily look for old stuff that would have a high silver content, but I also set aside anything that looks like it has a different design, even if it isn't particularly old, so I can look it up later. 

It was during one of these sorting sessions that I picked out a peculiar quarter, looked it up, and realized I had found a rare "Large Bust Mountie." Identifying that variety on my own, just by picking it out of the pile, was an incredibly cool feeling. I don't necessarily have a "white whale" coin I'm aggressively hunting for—I'm not as intense about coins as I am about things like baseball cards—but the quiet joy of discovery keeps me engaged.

These days, I am still actively listing, identifying, and archiving my collection. It's a slow and meticulous process, but it's one that brings me a lot of quiet joy. Every time I catalogue a new piece or spot a subtle die crack I hadn't noticed before, I feel a renewed connection to this hobby that started with a simple set of coins from 1993. It's an ongoing journey of discovery, preserving little pieces of history one coin at a time.
