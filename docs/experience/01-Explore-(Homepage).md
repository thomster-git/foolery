# 01 - Explore (Homepage)

> **Defining the landing experience and primary entry point into Project Atlas.**

---

# Purpose

The Explore page is the front door to Atlas.

Its purpose is not simply to introduce the website.

Its purpose is to invite curiosity.

Every visitor should quickly understand:

- What Atlas is.
- Why it exists.
- How to begin exploring.
- Why it is different from a traditional website.

The homepage should encourage discovery rather than direct visitors toward a predetermined destination.

---

# Visitor Goals

Within the first thirty seconds, visitors should understand:

- Atlas is a personal knowledge ecosystem.
- Content is connected rather than isolated.
- There are multiple ways to explore.
- The experience rewards curiosity.

Visitors should feel invited rather than instructed.

---

# Primary Question

The homepage revolves around a single question.

> **How would you like to explore today?**

Rather than organizing content into categories, Atlas offers multiple journeys.

The visitor chooses how they wish to experience the website.

---

# Exploration Paths

## 🧠 Think

Explore through technology, engineering, systems, analysis, and learning.

Ideal for visitors looking for technical depth.

---

## 🎨 Feel

Explore through stories, creativity, nostalgia, music, photography, and reflection.

Ideal for visitors looking for personal experiences and creative work.

---

## ⚖️ Connect

Experience Atlas as it was designed.

Technology and creativity.

Facts and stories.

Logic and emotion.

The Connect path demonstrates how ideas strengthen one another.

This should be the recommended experience for first-time visitors.

---

## 🎲 Surprise Me

Take visitors somewhere unexpected.

The destination should always feel intentional rather than random.

This option should reinforce Atlas's core philosophy:

> Everything is connected.

---

# Page Objectives

The homepage should:

- Welcome new visitors.
- Explain Atlas.
- Encourage exploration.
- Showcase featured content.
- Demonstrate connected thinking.
- Invite return visits.

The homepage is not intended to present everything.

Its purpose is to begin the journey.

---

# Information Hierarchy

The Explore page should present information in the following order.

```
Navigation

↓

Hero

↓

How would you like to explore today?

↓

Featured Journeys

↓

Featured Topics

↓

Recently Added

↓

Featured Projects

↓

Random Discovery

↓

Quote or Thought

↓

Footer
```

Each section should naturally encourage movement to the next.

# Hero Section

The hero should immediately communicate the purpose of Atlas.

Potential content includes:

Headline:

> **Everything is connected.**

Supporting text:

Atlas is a digital home exploring technology, creativity, nostalgia, and personal growth through connected stories, projects, and ideas.

Primary action:

**Begin Exploring**

Secondary action:

**Learn About Atlas**

---

# Featured Journeys

Rather than displaying categories, Atlas should present experiences.

Possible journeys include:

- Building a Home Lab
- Growing Up with Music
- Understanding AuDHD
- Rediscovering Creativity
- Engineering Curiosity
- Collections Through Time

Journeys may combine multiple topics into a single experience.

---

# Featured Topics

Highlight a rotating selection of major Atlas topics.

Examples:

- Technology
- Music
- Photography
- Baseball
- Drawing
- Fitness
- Video Editing
- Home Lab
- AuDHD
- Collections

Topics should rotate over time to encourage repeat visits.

---

# Random Discovery

One section should intentionally introduce visitors to something unexpected.

Examples:

"You came for networking...

Have you ever wondered why Billy Talent changed how I think about engineering?"

Unexpected connections are the defining feature of Atlas.

---

# Desktop Experience

Desktop layouts should emphasize spaciousness and exploration.

Hover interactions should reveal relationships between topics.

Multiple sections may be visible simultaneously, allowing visitors to compare and connect ideas naturally.

---

# Mobile Experience

Mobile layouts should prioritize vertical exploration.

Cards should expand naturally.

Navigation should remain simple.

Interactions should rely on taps rather than hover effects.

The experience should feel equally intentional regardless of screen size.

---

# Accessibility

The homepage should support:

- Keyboard navigation.
- Screen readers.
- Reduced motion preferences.
- High contrast.
- Responsive layouts.
- Semantic HTML.

Every visitor should be able to comfortably begin exploring Atlas.

---

# Future Ideas

Potential future enhancements include:

- Interactive knowledge graph preview.
- Personalized exploration.
- Seasonal featured journeys.
- Reading streaks.
- Interactive timelines.
- AI-assisted recommendations.
- Recently explored topics.

These features should strengthen exploration without increasing unnecessary complexity.

---

# Open Questions

Questions to revisit during implementation:

- How should Featured Journeys be selected?
- How often should featured content rotate?
- What determines the Surprise Me destination?
- Should visitors be able to customize their homepage?
- Should returning visitors receive a different landing experience?

---

# Relationship to Atlas

The Explore page is the visitor's first experience with Atlas.

It translates the philosophy established in the Project Vision into an interface that encourages curiosity, supports meaningful discovery, and demonstrates that knowledge is best experienced through relationships rather than categories.

Every interaction should reinforce Atlas's guiding belief:

> **Everything is connected.**
