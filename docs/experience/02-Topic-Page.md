---

# Purpose

Topic Pages are the primary knowledge hubs within Atlas.

Rather than acting as simple category pages, they provide context, relationships, and pathways for exploration.

A Topic Page should help visitors understand not only what a topic contains, but also how it connects to the rest of Atlas.

Every Topic Page should encourage curiosity beyond the original subject.

---

# Design Philosophy

Topic Pages are built around understanding rather than organization.

They should answer three questions:

- What is this topic?
- Why does it matter?
- Where can I explore next?

Every Topic Page should reinforce Atlas's philosophy that knowledge becomes more valuable when its relationships are visible.

---

# Visitor Goals

After viewing a Topic Page, visitors should:

- Understand the scope of the topic.
- Discover featured content.
- See how the topic relates to other areas of Atlas.
- Find a clear path toward deeper exploration.

The page should never feel like a dead end.

---

# Information Hierarchy

The Topic Page should present information in the following order.

```text
Navigation

↓

Topic Hero

↓

Topic Overview

↓

Featured Articles

↓

Featured Projects

↓

Collections

↓

Timeline

↓

Related Topics

↓

Knowledge Connections

↓

Continue Exploring

↓

Footer
```

Each section should naturally lead visitors toward additional discovery.

---

# Hero Section

The hero introduces the topic.

Recommended content includes:

- Topic title
- Short description
- Hero image or illustration
- Key themes
- Number of related articles and projects
- Primary call to action

Example:

```
Photography

Capturing moments, learning creativity, and documenting experiences.

18 Articles

4 Projects

7 Related Topics
```

The hero should immediately communicate both the subject and its significance.

---

# Topic Overview

The overview provides context rather than detail.

It should explain:

- Why this topic exists within Atlas.
- How it has evolved over time.
- Why it is personally meaningful.
- What visitors can expect to discover.

This section should remain concise while encouraging further reading.

---

# Featured Content

Highlight the most important content associated with the topic.

Possible content includes:

- Articles
- Essays
- Guides
- Videos
- Galleries
- Projects

Featured content should prioritize quality and relevance over publication date.

---

# Projects

Projects provide practical examples of the topic in action.

Each project should include:

- Title
- Summary
- Status
- Technologies (if applicable)
- Related content

Projects demonstrate learning through application.

---

# Collections

Where appropriate, Topic Pages may include collections.

Examples include:

- Baseball cards
- Coins
- Trading cards
- Books
- Games
- Equipment

Collections should emphasize the stories behind the items rather than simply cataloguing them.

---

# Timeline

Many topics naturally evolve over time.

When appropriate, include a timeline showing:

- Important milestones
- Learning experiences
- Personal growth
- Major projects
- Turning points

Timelines help visitors understand progression rather than isolated events.

---

# Knowledge Connections

This is the defining feature of Atlas.

Every Topic Page should explain how the current topic connects to other parts of the project.

Connections should include short explanations rather than simple links.

Example:

```
Photography

↓

Video Editing

Learning visual storytelling.

↓

Drawing

Developing composition skills.

↓

Technology

Understanding cameras and editing software.

↓

Travel

Capturing meaningful experiences.

↓

Creativity

Finding new ways to see familiar places.
```

Visitors should understand *why* these relationships exist.

---

# Related Topics

Display topics with strong relationships.

Examples:

- Music
- Drawing
- Video Editing
- Home Lab
- Technology
- AuDHD
- Engineering
- Collections

Relationships should be based on meaningful connections rather than shared tags.

---

# Continue Exploring

Every Topic Page should conclude with recommendations for further exploration.

Examples:

- Continue this story.
- Explore another perspective.
- Discover something unexpected.
- Follow the timeline.
- Read the related project.

The page should always encourage the next step.

---

# Desktop Experience

Desktop layouts should display relationships visually whenever practical.

Multiple sections may be visible simultaneously.

Knowledge connections should encourage lateral exploration.

---

# Mobile Experience

Mobile layouts should prioritize readability.

Sections should collapse naturally while maintaining clear navigation.

Relationship cards should remain easy to browse with touch interactions.

---

# Accessibility

Topic Pages should support:

- Semantic headings
- Keyboard navigation
- Screen readers
- High contrast
- Responsive layouts
- Reduced motion preferences

Accessibility should be considered throughout the design process rather than added afterward.

---

# Future Enhancements

Potential future features include:

- Interactive timelines
- Dynamic relationship maps
- Personalized recommendations
- AI-assisted topic summaries
- Reading progress
- Saved exploration paths
- Community-curated collections

Future additions should strengthen exploration without introducing unnecessary complexity.

---

# Open Questions

Questions for future development include:

- How many featured articles should appear?
- Should Topic Pages adapt based on visitor history?
- How should knowledge connections be visualized?
- Should timelines be interactive?
- How should large topics be organized as Atlas grows?

These questions should be revisited during implementation and future iterations.

---

# Relationship to Atlas

Topic Pages transform Atlas's philosophy into navigable knowledge hubs.

They connect stories, projects, media, and ideas into cohesive experiences that encourage visitors to explore beyond their original destination.

Every Topic Page should demonstrate that understanding grows through relationships.

> **Everything is connected.**
