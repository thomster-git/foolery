# Purpose

This document defines the ideal visitor journey through Atlas.

Rather than describing individual webpages, it describes the complete experience of exploration.

Every page, interaction, animation, and navigation decision should support this journey.

The goal is not simply to help visitors find information.

The goal is to help them discover meaningful connections.

---

# Experience Philosophy

Atlas should feel less like browsing a website and more like exploring a museum, library, or workshop.

Visitors should feel guided, but never constrained.

Every interaction should invite curiosity.

Every page should suggest another path worth exploring.

A successful visit is one where the visitor discovers something they were not originally looking for.

---

# Visitor Types

Atlas is designed for several kinds of visitors.

### Curious Explorer

Has no specific destination.

Wants to browse and discover.

Enjoys following unexpected connections.

---

### Focused Learner

Arrives looking for a particular topic.

Examples:

- AuDHD
- Home Lab
- Baseball
- Music
- Project Atlas

Should quickly find what they came for while naturally discovering related ideas.

---

### Returning Visitor

Already familiar with Atlas.

Returns to continue exploring, read new content, revisit older stories, or follow ongoing projects.

Should immediately see what's new without losing access to familiar paths.

---

### Friend or Colleague

Arrives through a shared article or direct link.

May know little about Atlas.

Should quickly understand what Atlas is and why it exists.

---

# The Ideal Journey

The ideal Atlas experience looks something like this.

```text
Landing

↓

How would you like to explore today?

↓

Think
Feel
Connect
Surprise Me

↓

Topic

↓

Article or Project

↓

Related Ideas

↓

Another Topic

↓

Collection

↓

Continue Exploring

↓

Return Later
```

The visitor is never forced down a single path.

Instead, every interaction opens new opportunities for exploration.

---

# Entry Points

Visitors may begin their journey in many ways.

Examples include:

- Homepage
- Search engine
- Shared article
- YouTube description
- Social media
- GitHub repository
- Direct link
- Bookmark

Every page should function as a potential front door to Atlas.

No page should assume the visitor has already seen the homepage.

---

# Homepage Experience

The homepage should answer four questions within the first few seconds.

1. What is Atlas?

2. Why should I explore it?

3. How do I begin?

4. Where might curiosity take me next?

Rather than presenting a list of categories, the homepage should encourage exploration through multiple paths.

---

# Exploration Paths

Visitors should be able to begin exploring through different perspectives.

## 🧠 Think

Technology.

Engineering.

Analysis.

Systems.

Learning.

Ideal for visitors seeking technical depth.

---

## 🎨 Feel

Stories.

Creativity.

Photography.

Music.

Reflection.

Ideal for visitors seeking personal experiences and creative work.

---

## ⚖️ Connect

The default Atlas experience.

Balances technical understanding with storytelling.

Shows how seemingly unrelated ideas strengthen one another.

---

## 🎲 Surprise Me

Introduces visitors to an unexpected part of Atlas.

This option embodies the philosophy that everything is connected.

It should always feel intentional rather than random.

---

# Discovery Loop

Atlas is designed around a continuous cycle of discovery.

```text
Discover

↓

Learn

↓

Connect

↓

Explore

↓

Discover Again
```

Every page should contribute to this loop.

The journey should never feel like a dead end.

---

# Page Responsibilities

Every page within Atlas should answer three questions.

### Why am I here?

The page clearly communicates its purpose.

---

### What should I learn?

The page provides valuable information, stories, or experiences.

---

### Where should I go next?

The page offers meaningful opportunities for continued exploration.

No page should feel isolated.

---

# Navigation Principles

Navigation should support curiosity.

Visitors should always understand:

- Where they are.
- What they are viewing.
- What is related.
- Where they can explore next.

Navigation should prioritize exploration over efficiency.

---

# Moments of Delight

Atlas should include small experiences that reward exploration.

Examples may include:

- Unexpected relationships between topics.
- Dynamic "Continue Exploring" suggestions.
- Rotating featured content.
- Thought-provoking quotes.
- Timeline discoveries.
- Interactive knowledge maps.
- Easter eggs celebrating long-time visitors.

These moments should feel earned rather than distracting.

---

# Success Criteria

A successful visitor journey is one where:

- Visitors explore multiple pages.
- They discover topics they did not expect.
- They understand the connections between ideas.
- They remember the experience.
- They choose to return.

Success is measured by curiosity rather than clicks.

---

# Open Questions

Future iterations should explore questions such as:

- How should "Surprise Me" choose content?
- Should visitors be able to save exploration paths?
- How should recommendations evolve over time?
- Should exploration become personalized?
- How should the knowledge graph integrate with navigation?

These questions should be revisited as Atlas evolves.

---

# Relationship to Atlas

The User Journey transforms the Atlas Blueprint into a visitor experience.

It defines how people move through Atlas, how curiosity is encouraged, and how meaningful connections are discovered.

Every page, interaction, and feature should support one central idea:

> **Everything is connected.**
