# Purpose

The Knowledge Model defines the conceptual building blocks of Atlas.

Rather than describing pages or software, it describes the knowledge itself.

Every article, project, photograph, collection, story, and relationship exists within this model.

The Knowledge Model ensures that Atlas grows as a connected ecosystem rather than a collection of isolated webpages.

---

# Philosophy

Atlas is built upon one simple belief.

> **Everything is connected.**

Knowledge becomes more valuable when its relationships are visible.

Rather than organizing information into rigid hierarchies, Atlas models ideas as an interconnected network.

Visitors should be able to explore naturally, moving between topics, themes, projects, and stories through meaningful relationships.

---

# Three Dimensions of Knowledge

Every piece of knowledge within Atlas can be described through three dimensions.

## 1. Topic

**What is this about?**

Examples include:

- Music
- Photography
- Baseball
- AuDHD
- Home Lab
- Drawing
- Video Editing
- Fitness

Topics describe the subject matter.

---

## 2. Content Type

**What kind of thing is it?**

Examples include:

- Article
- Project
- Collection
- Gallery
- Guide
- Essay
- Timeline
- Video
- Review

Content Types describe how information is presented.

---

## 3. Theme

**Why does it matter?**

Examples include:

- Curiosity
- Growth
- Creativity
- Identity
- Nostalgia
- Craftsmanship
- Learning
- Resilience
- Community
- Exploration

Themes describe the deeper ideas explored throughout Atlas.

Unlike Topics, Themes intentionally connect unrelated subject areas through shared meaning.

---

# Core Knowledge Objects

Atlas is built from several core object types.

## Topics

Topics organize knowledge around a subject.

Each Topic may contain:

- Articles
- Projects
- Collections
- Media
- Timelines

Topics also relate to other Topics through shared Themes.

---

## Articles

Articles communicate ideas, stories, experiences, or lessons.

Every Article belongs to one or more Topics.

Every Article should also reference one or more Themes.

Articles serve as the primary storytelling medium within Atlas.

---

## Projects

Projects document work that has been designed, built, or explored.

Projects demonstrate learning through application.

Projects often connect technical knowledge with personal experiences.

---

## Collections

Collections group meaningful objects.

Examples include:

- Baseball cards
- Trading cards
- Coins
- Video games
- Books
- Instruments

Collections emphasize the stories behind the items rather than simply cataloguing them.

---

## Media

Media supports understanding.

Examples include:

- Photographs
- Videos
- Audio
- Diagrams
- Illustrations

Media should reinforce the surrounding content rather than exist independently.

---

## Themes

Themes connect knowledge across Atlas.

A Theme may relate to dozens of Topics.

Examples:

Growth may connect:

- Music
- Fitness
- Home Lab
- Drawing
- Photography

Themes are one of Atlas's defining concepts.

---

# Relationships

Relationships are first-class citizens within Atlas.

Relationships should explain **why** two pieces of knowledge are connected.

Relationship types may include:

- Related Topic
- Shared Theme
- Inspiration
- Continuation
- Prerequisite
- Similar Experience
- Shared Technology
- Shared Skill
- Historical Connection
- Personal Connection

Relationships should always be meaningful rather than automatic.

---

# Knowledge Graph

Rather than existing within isolated folders, every object participates in a larger knowledge graph.

Example:

```text
Photography
      │
      ├── Creativity
      │
      ├── Video Editing
      │
      ├── Drawing
      │
      ├── Travel
      │
      └── Personal Growth
```

The graph is conceptual.

The implementation may vary over time.

---

# Discovery

Visitors should be able to discover knowledge through multiple paths.

Examples include:

- Topic
- Theme
- Project
- Collection
- Timeline
- Search
- Recommendations
- Surprise Me

Every object should support multiple methods of discovery.

---

# Context

Knowledge should never exist without context.

Every object should help answer:

- What is this?
- Why does it matter?
- How does it connect?
- Where should I explore next?

Context transforms isolated information into meaningful understanding.

---

# Growth

Atlas is designed to evolve.

New Topics, Themes, Content Types, and Relationship Types may be introduced as the project expands.

The Knowledge Model should remain flexible enough to accommodate future ideas without requiring fundamental redesign.

---

# Design Implications

The Knowledge Model influences every aspect of Atlas.

It informs:

- Navigation
- Search
- Recommendations
- Metadata
- Content Strategy
- Wireframes
- User Experience
- Software Architecture

Every system should reinforce meaningful exploration.

---

# Future Considerations

Future versions of Atlas may expand the Knowledge Model with concepts such as:

- People
- Places
- Events
- Technologies
- Skills
- Organizations
- Series
- Learning Paths

These additions should strengthen the network of knowledge without increasing unnecessary complexity.

---

# Relationship to Atlas

The Knowledge Model defines the conceptual foundation of Atlas.

It explains what knowledge exists, how it is categorized, and how relationships create meaning across the project.

The Technical Architecture defines how Atlas is built.

The Content Strategy defines how knowledge is created.

The Knowledge Model defines what knowledge **is**.

The Knowledge Model is implemented by Atlas Core through the Data Model and Content Schema.

Every object within Atlas should reinforce the project's guiding belief:

> **Everything is connected.**
