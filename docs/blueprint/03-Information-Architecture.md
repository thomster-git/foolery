# Information Philosophy

Project Atlas is not designed around pages.

It is designed around relationships.

Rather than organizing information into isolated categories, Atlas encourages exploration through meaningful connections between ideas, stories, projects, experiences, and media.

Every piece of content should naturally lead to another.

Visitors should feel as though they are exploring a living network of ideas rather than navigating a collection of webpages.

Knowledge should flow naturally, rewarding curiosity and encouraging discovery.

---

# Content Model

The fundamental building block of Atlas is the **Node**.

A node represents a single piece of knowledge, regardless of its format.

Examples include:

- Article
- Project
- Collection
- Gallery
- Video
- Timeline
- Person
- Place
- Tool
- Series

Every node exists independently while maintaining relationships with other nodes throughout the website.

The goal is to create a network of knowledge rather than a hierarchy of pages.

---

# Site Hierarchy

Atlas uses a hybrid approach.

Traditional navigation exists to provide orientation.

Relationships exist to encourage exploration.

```
Home
│
├── Explore
├── Topics
├── Projects
├── Collections
├── About
└── Search
```

The hierarchy helps visitors understand where they are.

Relationships help visitors decide where to go next.

---

# Knowledge Graph

Relationships are the defining feature of Atlas.

Every node should connect to related content whenever meaningful relationships exist.

Relationships may be based on:

- Shared topics
- Common themes
- Technologies
- People
- Places
- Time periods
- Skills
- Lessons learned
- Personal experiences
- Related projects

For example:

```
Billy Talent
    │
    ├── Guitar
    ├── Green Day
    ├── Bo Burnham
    ├── Mental Health
    ├── AuDHD
    ├── Concert Photography
    ├── Video Essays
    └── Burnout
```

Connections are not decorative.

They are fundamental to how Atlas is experienced.

---

# Navigation Strategy

Navigation should prioritize exploration over efficiency.

Rather than immediately asking visitors what they want to read, Atlas first asks:

> **How would you like to explore today?**

Visitors may choose to explore through different perspectives.

### 🧠 Think

Technology.

Engineering.

Analysis.

Systems.

Learning.

---

### 🎨 Feel

Stories.

Creativity.

Memories.

Photography.

Music.

Reflection.

---

### ⚖️ Connect

The intersection of logic and creativity.

This is the default Atlas experience, where technical understanding and personal storytelling reinforce one another.

---

### 🎲 Surprise Me

A guided journey through unexpected connections.

This option intentionally embraces curiosity by introducing visitors to topics they may never have searched for directly.

---

# Discovery Methods

Visitors should be able to discover content in multiple ways.

Examples include:

- Topics
- Themes
- Projects
- Collections
- Timeline
- Technologies
- Skills
- Related Stories
- Related Projects
- Lens (Think, Feel, Connect)
- Random Discovery
- Search

No single method should become the "correct" way to navigate Atlas.

Exploration should always remain flexible.

---

# Search

Search should prioritize relevance over exact matches.

Results should consider:

- Titles
- Summaries
- Full content
- Topics
- Themes
- Tags
- Related content
- Projects
- Collections

Search should also encourage discovery by recommending related content alongside direct matches.

---

# Relationships

Every node should actively connect to other nodes.

Possible relationship types include:

- Related Topics
- Related Projects
- Shared Technologies
- Shared Skills
- Shared Experiences
- Similar Stories
- Recommended Reading
- Continue Exploring

Relationships should help visitors understand *why* two pieces of content are connected rather than merely listing similar tags.

---

# Metadata

Every node should contain rich metadata to support search, filtering, and relationships.

Recommended metadata includes:

- Title
- Summary
- Content Type
- Topics
- Themes
- Tags
- Lens
- Difficulty
- Series
- People
- Places
- Technologies
- Skills
- Related Content
- Created Date
- Updated Date

Metadata should exist to improve discovery rather than satisfy technical requirements.

---

# URL Structure

URLs should remain clean, readable, and stable.

Examples:

```
/music/billy-talent

/projects/project-atlas

/health/autistic-burnout

/collections/baseball-cards
```

URLs should describe content clearly without exposing implementation details.

---

# Future Expansion

Atlas is designed to grow over many years.

The information architecture should support future content types without requiring structural redesign.

Potential future additions include:

- Interactive timelines
- Knowledge maps
- Dynamic relationship graphs
- Learning paths
- Reading lists
- Media libraries
- Collections
- Personal journals
- Community features (if ever introduced)

Growth should strengthen the existing network of knowledge rather than create isolated sections.

---

# Relationship to Atlas

The Information Architecture transforms the philosophy established in the Project Vision into a practical framework for organizing knowledge.

It defines how visitors experience Atlas, discover relationships, and navigate the growing network of stories, projects, and ideas.

Every structural decision should reinforce Atlas's core belief:

> **Everything is connected.**
